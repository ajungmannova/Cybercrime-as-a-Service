# Cybercrime-as-a-Service

This thesis provides a comprehensive, cross-market analysis of Cybercrime-as-a-Service  (CaaS) offerings across multiple dark web marketplaces, establishing its central role in the cybercrime economy. Making tools and services for illicit cybercrime operations readily available lowers technical barriers for would-be offenders. Despite growing scholarly interest in cybercrime and its economy, systematic comparative studies of CaaS are sparse. This thesis addresses this empirical gap by investigating the marketplace organization, category composition, pricing strategies, and cross-platform vendor activity across three dark web markets: Abacus, Nexus, and Vortex.

The empirical research relies on original open-source intelligence (OSINT) data systematically collected at three-day intervals between May and June 2025. To enable meaningful cross-market comparison, a custom data pipeline was developed to ingest, standardize, and manually recategorize listings via a standardized 11-category coding scheme validated through Inter-Coder Reliability testing.

The findings reveal that the contemporary CaaS landscape operates not as a single unified market, but as a fragmented yet interconnected ecosystem of parallel marketplaces. Although malware and exploit tools dominate the overall listing volume, individual marketplaces have their own distinct niches. Specifically, Nexus functions as an advanced, infrastructure-heavy business-to-business (B2B) platform; Vortex as a lightweight repository specializing in commodity data leaks and credential dumps rather than complex technical infrastructure; and Abacus as a discount storefront for entry-level tools. Furthermore, pricing analysis shows that dark web prices reflect how easily a digital product can be replicated rather than the potential damage it can cause to a victim. Finally, the stability of median prices over time, combined with the methods top vendors manipulate prices across different platforms, is consistent with a more industrialized and increasingly professionalized market structure.
