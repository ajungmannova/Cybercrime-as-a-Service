# Cybercrime-as-a-Service

## Abstract

This thesis compares Cybercrime-as-a-Service (CaaS) offerings across three dark web marketplaces, Abacus, Nexus, and Vortex, observed in parallel in mid-2025. Ready-made tools and services lower the technical barriers to cybercrime, yet recent evidence that tracks several active marketplaces simultaneously, using a single category scheme and repeated snapshots, remains scarce.

Listings from the CaaS-related sections of the three marketplaces were collected every three days between 26 May and 28 June 2025 using custom scrapers run over Tor. The resulting dataset of 14,006 listing observations, covering 1,609 unique products offered by 74 vendors, was manually coded into 11 categories (Cohen's κ=.678 when compared with an independent coder). Prices were converted to US dollars at daily ECB reference rates, and price differences were tested both at the listing level and with one value per vendor.

Malware and exploitation tools were the largest category on every marketplace (40–52% of unique products in the collected sections), while category diversity was similar across markets. Service-intensive offerings such as hacking-for-hire were priced well above guides and other easily replicated goods. Cross-market price differences were significant in 10 of 11 categories at the listing level, but almost all disappeared at the vendor level, indicating that they reflect where vendors sell. Listed prices were nearly fixed: only 3.05% of products changed price, mostly due to one vendor's currency switch. Twenty-one vendors were active on more than one marketplace; they ran larger catalogs (median 32 vs 5 listings) and kept identical prices for 63.6% of products listed across several markets. Almost half of all vendor handles also appear on an earlier generation of marketplaces. Before Abacus closed in July 2025, its catalog stopped changing, and major vendors withdrew. The CaaS supply observed is a fragmented but connected set of marketplaces selling standardized, fixed-price products, shaped more by vendors than by platforms.
